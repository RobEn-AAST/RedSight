## 2. Challenge brief (Martian Map — official wording)

- **Core (must):** "Your challenge is to create a map, tool, or interface a Mars astronaut could use during a surface exploration mission, using NASA data or tools." — **[Challenge page]**
- **Integrated view:** "…a layered, integrated view of a location or route on the Martian surface that pulls together data from multiple NASA science missions and could help a human explorer plan and carry out a successful Marswalk…" — **[Challenge page]** excerpt
- **Recommended:** "You may consider including at least three kinds of data—optical, thermal, spectral, topographic, geophysical, atmospheric, radiation, and chemical are a few examples—layered or combined…" — **[Challenge page]**
- **Recommended:** astronaut-safety framing; "if possible, include a route map that shows where the astronaut will travel… while avoiding significant obstacles" — **[Challenge page]**
- Page's example starting point: science goals + location (boxwork → Mount Sharp). Our specific site choice = **[Team note]**, not a requirement.
- **Explicitly optional** ("You may (but are not required to) consider the following:"): habitat/tablet/HUD formats; static vs interactive vs map-generator; other-world map inspiration; past/future/no time frame; POV rover imagery; rovers or paired astronauts. **None are required.** — **[Challenge page]**
- Category "Intermediate". — **[Challenge page]**

## 4. Project work rules

- **No pre-work:** "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon." — **[NASA global]** FAQs (boundary → open-questions #3)
- **SI/AI — allowed with mandatory rules** — **[NASA global]** Submission Guide "Use of Super Intelligence (SI)":
  - Answer the SI question on the project page (how tools were used).
  - SI "should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)."
  - Generated content may **not use/contain/modify NASA branding or design elements** (logos, flags, mission identifiers — per https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/).
  - **Images/videos: visible watermark** indicating SI generation; audio/code/data: acknowledge in descriptive text and metadata.
  - The SI answer itself isn't shown to judges, but visible SI indicators are, and judges evaluate originality/intent/execution.
- **Minors' media ban:** no images, video, audio, or likeness of under-18s in the demo or project, per the Terms & Conditions (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/). — **[NASA global]** Submission Guide ✅ (Cairo file §1.1 adds "names" — follow the stricter wording)
- One team, one challenge. — **[NASA global]** FAQs

## 6. Judging

- **✅ VERIFIED five criteria — [NASA global]** Judging Guide (confirms Cairo file §4): "At every level of judging, from Local and Universal Events to Executive Judging, your project will be evaluated on a scale of 1-to-5" in:
  1. **Impact** — significance of problem, reach/benefit.
  2. **Creativity** — originality/novelty vs existing ideas.
  3. **Validity** — scientifically sound, technically feasible, usable; fully-developed vs refinement-needed matters.
  4. **Relevance** — engages the challenge's goals; "NASA data is integral to the project's success."
  5. **Presentation** — story/structure/clarity; "Judges evaluate the demo and full project to compute this score."
- **Phases & dates:** Local judges (recruited by the Local Lead, same criteria) select **Global Nominees — announced Dec 3, 2026** → Global Judges select **Global Finalists + Honorable Mentions — Dec 17, 2026** → NASA/Space-Agency executive committee selects Global Winners (Jan 2027 per FAQs) for **ten Global Awards** (incl. Galactic Impact, Global Connection, Local Impact). Local awards (e.g., Cairo's People's Choice) are separate from Global Judging. — **[NASA global]** Judging Guide + FAQs
