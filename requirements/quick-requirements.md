## Step-by-step checklist

1. [ ] **Confirm participation and submission authority.** Identify the team leader as the sole submitter; obtain the original submission instructions and deadline before final submission. [C §2] says only the leader submits, late entries are rejected, and submitted material cannot be changed. Eligibility, registration requirements, and the actual cutoff are not stated in [C]; do not infer them.
2. [ ] **Demonstrate challenge fit.** State the selected challenge, “Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map,” and connect the problem and feasible proposed solution to its objectives. [C §3; §4 Relevance] requires addressing a NASA challenge; [R title/source] supplies this challenge name, but not the full challenge objectives.
3. [ ] **Show meaningful NASA resource use.** Record each selected NASA resource, its source link, its actual contribution, and applicable use terms. [C §3; §4 Relevance] calls for meaningful NASA data/resource use; [C §2] requires source credit and copyright compliance. [R] lists Mars Trek, the USGS-NASA mapping program, the Curiosity Mount Sharp route reference, Worldview, and Perseverance images; listing them alone does not demonstrate use. [R] also relays a requirement to comply with non-government sites' data-use parameters.
4. [ ] **Collect build and validity evidence.** Explain how the solution works and attach reliable results, tests, prototype, model, or demo evidence; distinguish demonstrated behavior from proposed behavior. [C §4 Validity] requires scientific/technical soundness, feasibility, evidence, and justified claims; it does not specify a particular implementation stack or test suite.
5. [ ] **Prepare the permitted demo.** Choose a **7-slide presentation OR a 30-second video** and place it on a publicly accessible platform; exclude images, names, and voices of anyone under 18. These are claims of [C §1.1], pending confirmation against the missing original guide.
6. [ ] **Assemble project links.** Include the final project link, GitHub repository, and relevant development/code links. [C §1.2] lists these deliverables; confirm what qualifies as the final project link in the original instructions.
7. [ ] **Write the short summary.** Cover the problem, solution, impact, and relationship to the selected challenge, with the project description and supporting materials ready. [C §1.3; §3] gives these requirements; no word limit is supplied.
8. [ ] **Complete resources, credits, and AI disclosure.** List datasets, sources, references, tools, and source credits; check copyright and content requirements. Follow the original AI-use disclosure instructions once obtained; [C §1.4; §2–3] requires disclosure compliance but supplies no form, wording, or field list.
9. [ ] **Review all five judging criteria.** For **Impact**, explain beneficiaries and value; for **Creativity**, explain the original contribution; for **Validity**, show feasibility and evidence; for **Relevance**, tie the challenge objectives to meaningful NASA use; for **Presentation**, clearly organize problem, method, solution, results, and impact. These are [C §4] criteria; weights and scoring scales are not supplied.
10. [ ] **Run the final link and content check before the leader submits.** Open every submitted link without a signed-in account and confirm no password, registration, or access request is needed; check final content against the original instructions, disclosure, and deadline. [C §2–3] requires public access and instruction compliance, rejects late entries, and forbids changes after submission. A signed-out check is a practical way to check that access claim, not an additional quoted rule. No links have been tested or submission made by this artifact task.
