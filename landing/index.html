W-01 // THE PROBLEM

          
One wrong
step.

          
The first astronauts on Mars will walk terrain no human has seen, on a
          fixed supply of oxygen, where a slope that is too steep or a walk that is too long
          can end the mission. NASA has mapped Gale crater in great detail, but its tools
          (Mars Trek, USGS maps) are built for regional and global views,
          not for the few square kilometres an astronaut actually walks.

W-02 // THE CONCEPT

        
One map.
Three ways to
rehearse a walk.

      

      

        

          
          
          
        

        

          

            

              
Plan the walk before the airlock opens

              
Answer five questions (crew, load, rover, goal, review), then open the map.
              For each stop RedSight shows the fastest and the lowest-hazard walking route,
              modeled time and O₂ out and back, the turn-back point, green / amber / red return
              zones, and where ridges cut the radio link to the habitat. Have a rover? It drives,
              parks 100 m out, and the crew walks the rest.

              
    
                
  • ROUTING — DIJKSTRA ON THE HIRISE DTM, 8 NEIGHBORS
    • 
                
  • ON FOOT — EVERY SLOPE OVER 15° IS BLOCKED
    • 
                
  • OUTPUT — TIME · O₂ · TURN-BACK POINT · RADIO SHADOW
    • 
              

            

            

              
            

          

          

            

              
Stand at the stop, inside the helmet

              
At each stop, step into a real 360° panorama Curiosity took on that spot,
              with a see-through visor HUD on top: a heading-up radar of your route that turns
              with your view, O₂, the way home, and the radio delay to Earth. The crew sees the
              ground before they walk it.

              
    
                
  • REAL CURIOSITY 360° PANORAMAS (NASA PIA)
    • 
                
  • HEADING-UP ROUTE RADAR · O₂ · WAY HOME
    • 
                
  • EARTH ↔ MARS RADIO DELAY
    • 
              

            

            

              
            

          

          

            

              
Play the plan, then break it

              
Press play and two astronauts walk the plan on the map. An O₂ gauge counts down
              with a reserve that is never planned, a callout comes every 10 %, and the turn-back
              moment is marked. Run a drill (suit leak, hard work) to see how the plan holds up.
              A voice copilot answers by speech or text, can set up the mission for you, and
              explains every number.

              
    
                
  • MISSION REPLAY + DRILLS
    • 
                
  • 25 % O₂ RESERVE, NEVER PLANNED
    • 
                
  • VOICE COPILOT — SPEECH IN, SPEECH OUT
    •

W-03 // THE SCIENCE

        
Every number
has a source.

        
The routes come from our own Python engine running on a HiRISE elevation
        model of the site. Time and O₂ come from a published load-carriage model moved to Mars
        gravity, with a suit penalty. Every layer and constant in the app is labeled
        Observed, Derived or Scenario, and the
        numbers are model estimates, never guarantees.

      

      

        

          
COST MODEL — PER TRAVERSE STEP

          
blocked if slope > 15° on foot, or no terrain data

fastest        = Dijkstra, cost = walking time
lowest-hazard  = Dijkstra, cost = d × (1 + slope²)

P   = Pandolf(M, L, v, grade) at g = 3.71 × κ (suit)
O₂  = P × 2.9 mL/min per W × minutes
turn back when  O₂ used + 1.2 × O₂ home > budget

          
BUDGET = 385 L USABLE × 75 % (25 % RESERVE) · PER ASTRONAUT
